# Seven different days · June 2–8

Open Yearbound → Explore the calendar → June → choose 2–8. These seven compact decoration reference routes join the original six sample days. All thirteen are available immediately. Each new day has its own original pixel-art landscape, authored scenery, terrain palette and supplied MP3. They retain the 48-pixel block grid, continuous floor and existing movement.

The source material was the music, MusicXML and musical guides in the user's Documents/Yearbound folder. Those files were treated as creative references. Original files were left intact; the supplied Python composition/rendering scripts were not run.

The 8 June folder did not contain a musical guide. Its scene is an interpretation of the supplied MusicXML work title, “Apricot Lanterns in the Breeze,” and score directions including a lift on beat two and a spacious cantabile. No storyline is assigned to these places.

## Readability and movement

Collision terrain keeps full square cells, dark borders and bright top surfaces. High-detail landscape paintings are graded into the distance; the new props draw in the rear scenery layer. Flowers are moved or shortened near spike patches. Tall scenery, water and lantern strings never add collision. Reduced motion freezes decorative animation. Existing movement tuning, seasonal sample mechanics and the monthly boss are retained.

Each new route has individually authored block positions, optional upper routes, sunmotes, two checkpoint lanterns and an exit. These are compact design references, not finished five-minute stages. Section names guide spatial composition; they are not synchronized to soundtrack timestamps. The full supplied MP3s play from the beginning on stage entry and restart after their natural endings if the player remains in the level. The music has not been cut into seamless loops.

## Reuse a design in the workshop

Choose **Copy a sample day…**, select any of the new dates, edit the blocks and **Playtest**. The workshop preserves the day-specific background, atmosphere, scenery and music in exported layouts. All thirteen installed tracks are selectable. The layout canvas uses simplified decoration markers; Playtest shows the finished art. Specialized props can be repositioned or expanded in JSON; the initial brush palette still offers the general tree and flower brushes.

**New** chooses the next unwritten date (18 June as of 0.11.0). Existing user drafts retain their saved date. Exported JSON can be sent back for incorporation; replacing an installed date requires the installer's explicit `--replace` flag and creates a backup.

## Content contract for future days

- `content/stages/06-02.json` through `06-08.json` hold complete, independent layouts and decoration arrays.
- `identity.key` is the unique design identifier; `identity.sections` records the local composition sequence. Treat this as a day brief, not a procedural recolouring recipe.
- `background` points to the day's landscape under `art/june/`. `ambience` controls `haze`, `haze_strength` and `separation` independently of the seasonal theme. Strengths are in the range 0–1.
- `terrain_style` selects a palette from `content/terrain_styles.json`. Tile geometry and collision remain shared and predictable.
- `scripts/day_decor.gd` supplies the new animated scenic vocabulary. Each prop has `type`, `x`, `y`, optional `width`, `height` and `phase`. Props belong behind the gameplay. Add new vocabulary when a day's identity demands it.
- `music` points to the copied source MP3. `identity.music_source` and `identity.music_sha256` record its provenance.
- `content/catalog.json` registers available dates. `stages` defines campaign order; `featured` retains six seasonal shortcuts. The workshop discovers installed dates and tracks from this catalog.

Original backgrounds were generated with the built-in image-generation tool. The exact prompts, source output paths, shipped resource paths and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `art/june/PROVENANCE.json`. Animation and nearby props are native drawing code. No Towerverse or Celeste artwork was copied.

Before adding another day, define a different place, landmark sequence, shape language, light and motion. Shared tiles support readable movement; recurring scenery should be an intentional callback, not a substitute for a new day's design.
